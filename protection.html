Student Protection & Complaint Review

A structured email-based process for evidence review, right of reply, mediation, member accountability and appropriate escalation.

What may be reported

Financial misconduct

Payment taken without promised work, disputed refunds, concealed charges or suspected misappropriation.

False claims

Fabricated admission, scholarship or visa promises; misleading university information; or material facts intentionally hidden.

Document misconduct

Forged documents, unauthorized applications, misuse of student information or suspicious alterations.

Serious professional misconduct

Threats, harassment, coercion or other conduct that may materially harm a student.

How CBCA handles a complaint

1. Intake and reference

The Secretariat checks whether the submission contains enough information and assigns a case reference when accepted.

2. Evidence review

Receipts, agreements, emails, messages and relevant records are reviewed. A complaint is not proof of guilt.

3. Right of reply

The concerned person or organization receives a fair opportunity to respond and submit evidence.

4. Mediation or member action

CBCA may facilitate a voluntary resolution. Proven misconduct by a member may lead to warning, corrective direction, suspension or cancellation under approved rules.

5. External referral

Potential fraud, forgery, misappropriation or other legal issues may be referred to the appropriate government, law-enforcement, consumer-rights or legal channel.

Legal boundary: CBCA is not a court, police agency or statutory regulator. It cannot compel compensation or declare criminal liability.

Prepare your evidence

  • Your name and contact details
  • Agency / person complained about
  • Transaction proof
  • Agreement or written promise
  • Relevant email or chat history
  • Timeline of events
  • Requested resolution

For initial submission, provide only the last four digits of a passport number. Do not email unnecessary sensitive identity documents.
